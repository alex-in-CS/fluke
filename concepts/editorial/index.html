How it works

A meeting, as
Fluke hears it.

  1. Them

    Thanks for making the time. Before we wrap, where did we land on the launch date?

  2. Question heard

    October 28. QA wraps on the 24th and the security review follows, which leaves a small buffer.

  3. You

    October 28. QA wraps on the 24th, so we keep a small buffer.

  4. Them

    Good. And pilot pricing, same as last quarter?

  5. From your notes, Oct 9

    Yes. You agreed to hold pilot pricing through the first quarter.

  6. You

    Same as last quarter, and we hold it through Q1.

Your microphone is You. System audio is Them. Each is transcribed on its own, so every line is attributed exactly. When the meeting ends, Fluke keeps the decisions for next time.

Your keys.

Paste a key from the provider you already pay for, or run a model locally. Keys live in the Windows credential vault and only the native core reads them.

Your machine.

No Fluke servers, no telemetry, no account. Requests go straight to your provider, and history is a file on your own disk.

Your call.

Let it answer the moment a question lands, or only when you press a key. Hide the overlay from screen sharing whenever you like.
